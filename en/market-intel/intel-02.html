This week

Taiwan’s FSC issues cyber-security strengthening measures: on 2026-11-26 the Financial Supervisory Commission set out measures based on the consensus of its 12 November CISO meeting, with four policy directions and 14 self-assessment items in five groups. Institutions must complete the self-assessment before the end of 2026 and submit it to their next board meeting.
  2. Bank of England, PRA and FCA consult on cyber-resilience guidance: the three authorities opened a consultation on 2026-11-19 that closes on 2027-02-26, and published effective-practice documents on cyber risk relating to AI and to quantum computing.
  3. Korea’s FSC sets two inspection deadlines: the Financial Services Commission announced a security overhaul for the whole financial sector on 2026-11-05. Checks of registered external access points are due by 30 November; a full survey of unregistered systems and internal controls is due by 31 December.

Taiwan

  • FSC cyber-security strengthening measures: 14 self-assessment items by year-end (FSC press release (in Chinese), 2026-11-26): the FSC said major cyber incidents had occurred in quick succession at home and abroad and that AI is accelerating attacks, so it issued the measures based on the consensus of the CISO meeting of 12 November 2026. The four policy directions are: get the basics right, minimise external exposure, apply zero trust to external-party access, and adopt multi-factor authentication, least privilege, network segmentation and sufficient monitoring; prioritise vulnerability patching by whether a system is externally exposed and exploitable; boards give security units sufficient resources; and evaluate defensive AI cautiously where the risk is controllable. The 14 self-assessment items fall into five groups: reducing exposure (inventory external connection points and close unnecessary services, patch exploited critical vulnerabilities first, set compensating controls and replacement plans for end-of-support systems); identity, key and third-party component control (MFA and least privilege for external staff and management interfaces, removal of idle accounts, management of non-human identities such as service accounts and API keys, and of certificates); data and transaction security (test for unauthorised access, limit personal-data queries and exports to business need, bring fraud derived from leaked data into transaction monitoring and customer alerts); detection, tracing and response (block or monitor reported indicators of compromise and trace back, confirm real-time monitoring works, assess response capacity, run drills that include AI-attack scenarios with management participating); and medium- to long-term strengthening (a zero-trust seven-element baseline in high-risk areas, evaluation of defensive AI for vulnerability detection and patching, threat intelligence and the security operations centre, with pilots planned). Institutions may adjust scope by system importance, exposure and whether personal data or funds are handled; equivalent alternatives need documented reasons and CISO approval; boards should prioritise budget and staffing and pre-authorise the CISO’s emergency powers. The press release states that institutions should complete the 14 self-assessment items before the end of 2026 and submit them to the most recent board meeting for review.
  • The six measures from the FSC CISO meeting (FSC press release (in Chinese), 2026-11-13): the FSC held its second 2026 CISO liaison meeting on 2026-11-12, covering financial cyber governance, security management of agentic AI, emerging AI attack risks and response to major incidents. The six measures are: know external exposure and patch critical vulnerabilities first, including software bills of materials; zero trust and least privilege for high-risk access, with anomaly detection and real-time blocking for sensitive data and trading operations; secure software development and cautious evaluation of defensive AI that uses AI against AI; use of F-ISAC threat intelligence and stronger joint defence across the third-party supply chain; continuity and rapid recovery of critical services, with drills covering AI-attack scenarios and reissuance of digital certificates; and board and senior-management responsibility for cyber governance with adequate resources. The 26 November measures build on these six.

Asia

  • Korea’s Financial Services Commission: security overhaul for the whole financial sector (FSC Korea press release (in Korean), 2026-11-05): after a meeting with the Financial Supervisory Service, the Financial Security Institute and the heads of the financial industry associations, the FSC published its plan, attached under the title “全금융권 보안체계 혁신방안”. The release says that from 30 September to 3 November, 37 companies reported 40 security incidents. The timetable is: checks of external access points listed in the management register to be completed by 30 November; a full survey of unregistered systems and of the internal-control system, including CEOs and CISOs, to be completed by 31 December; and a security internal-control guideline to be prepared and implemented in the first quarter of 2027.
  • MAS Guidelines on AI Risk Management take effect in October 2027 (MAS, 2026-10-07): the Monetary Authority of Singapore published its Guidelines on Artificial Intelligence Risk Management for Financial Institutions on 2026-10-07. They apply to all financial institutions and all forms of AI technology, and take effect on 2027-10-07.
  • Response deadline for the HKMA agentic-AI discussion paper (HKMA discussion paper, PDF, 2026-11): the HKMA’s Discussion Paper on Agentic A.I. Governance in the Banking Sector seeks views on intent-anchored accountability, the agent harness, human oversight and stop mechanisms, and a registry of material AI agents. The paper does not itself introduce binding supervisory requirements; first-round responses are due before 2027-02-01.

Elsewhere

  • Bank of England, PRA and FCA: cyber-resilience consultation and effective practices for AI and quantum (Bank of England, 2026-11-19): the Bank of England, the Prudential Regulation Authority and the FCA jointly published a consultation on cyber-resilience guidance, closing on 2027-02-26, together with two effective-practice documents on cyber risk and resilience management relating to AI and to quantum computing, which are not under formal consultation. The release cites the Bank and FCA’s 2026 survey of AI in UK financial services, in which 88% of respondents expect frontier-AI cyber capabilities to have a moderate or high impact on their risk profile over the next two years.
  • EBA publishes final revised Guidelines on internal governance (EBA press release, 2026-11-05): the European Banking Authority published the final revised Guidelines on internal governance (EBA/GL/2026/10), implementing CRD VI (Directive (EU) 2024/1619); the guidelines are final and awaiting translation into the EU official languages. The final report states that the guidelines apply from two months after publication in all EU official languages. It says internal governance encompasses sound information and communication technology, including the use of frontier AI and quantum technologies, and that references to risks in the guidelines include risks in the context of the use of AI and quantum technologies.
  • EU AI Act: the 2 December application date (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, EUR-Lex, published in the Official Journal 2026-07-24): under the amended Article 113, the new prohibitions added to Article 5 apply from 2026-12-02. Under a new paragraph 4 of Article 111, providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text and were placed on the market before 2026-08-02 must comply with the marking obligations in Article 50(2) by 2026-12-02. Obligations for high-risk AI systems listed in Annex III apply from 2027-12-02, and for those listed in Annex I from 2028-08-02.
  • Australia’s automated decision-making transparency obligation starts on 10 December (OAIC, 2026-09-30): the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner said that from 2026-12-10, entities covered by the Privacy Act must include information in their privacy policy where they have arranged for a computer program to make, or do something substantially and directly related to making, a decision that could significantly affect an individual’s rights or interests, where personal information is used in the process. The policy must state the kinds of personal information used, the kinds of decisions made solely by the program, and the kinds of decisions in which the program is substantially involved (APP 1.7 to 1.8). The obligation was introduced by the Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024. The OAIC published a fact sheet, a flowchart and an update to its APP 1 guidelines the same day, reflecting 90 written submissions received in consultation.
