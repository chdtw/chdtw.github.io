This week

  1. The EU AI Act’s 2 December application date: under Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, adopted in July 2026, the new prohibitions added to Article 5 apply from 2026-12-02; providers of AI systems that generate synthetic content and were placed on the market before 2026-08-02 must comply with the machine-readable marking obligation in Article 50(2) by 2026-12-02. High-risk obligations are deferred to 2027-12-02 for Annex III systems and 2028-08-02 for Annex I systems.
  2. BSI issues its 50th ISO/IEC 42001 certificate: in a press release published in November, the British Standards Institution said its 50th AI management system certificate was awarded on 2026-10-21 to UKG Inc. in the United States, and that certificates issued in 2026 have more than tripled compared with the whole of 2025 (11).
  3. DIFC becomes the first financial centre certified to ISO/IEC 42001: the Dubai International Financial Centre announced on 2026-11-17 that it had achieved ISO/IEC 42001:2023 certification across the AI User, AI Producer and AI Provider scopes.

Asia

  • Korea’s K Bank obtains ISO/IEC 42001 certification (Financial News, English edition, 2026-10-19): according to Korea’s Financial News, K Bank obtained ISO/IEC 42001 certification in September from DNV Business Assurance Korea, with a ceremony held on the 16th. The report describes the scope as a system for identifying and managing risks across AI services from planning and development to operation.

Elsewhere

  • Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 revises the AI Act timetable (EUR-Lex, published in the Official Journal 2026-07-24): the regulation known as the Digital Omnibus on AI was adopted on 2026-07-08 and entered into force on 2026-07-27. The amended Article 113 provides that the new points (ba) and (bb) of Article 5(1), and the new Article 5(1a) and (1b), apply from 2026-12-02; the recitals explain that the new prohibitions target AI systems that generate or manipulate non-consensual intimate material and child sexual abuse material. A new paragraph 4 of Article 111 requires providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text and were placed on the market before 2026-08-02 to comply with Article 50(2) by 2026-12-02, described in the recitals as a four-month transitional period. The obligations in Chapter III, Sections 1 to 3 apply from 2027-12-02 for high-risk systems listed in Annex III and from 2028-08-02 for those listed in Annex I. The regulation also requires the Commission to publish guidelines by 2027-08-01 on how high-risk AI systems covered by Annex I can comply with both the AI Act and the relevant Union harmonisation legislation. The recitals further state that the codes of practice referred to in Article 50(7) and Article 56(6) have limited legal effect and in particular do not grant a presumption of conformity.
  • The Commission’s Code of Practice on AI-generated content and Article 50 guidelines (European Commission: Code of Practice, Guidelines, 2026-06-10): the Commission’s timeline lists 2026-06-10 as the publication date of the final Code of Practice; the Article 50 transparency obligations apply from 2026-08-02. The text from the Code’s Working Group 1 requires marking and detection solutions to be effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as technically feasible. The guidelines page is titled “Guidelines on transparency obligations for providers and deployers of certain AI systems”.
  • BSI issues its 50th ISO/IEC 42001 certificate (BSI press release, 2026-10-21; the release was published in November without a printed date): BSI said the 50th certificate was awarded on 2026-10-21 to UKG Inc., headquartered in Lowell, Massachusetts, less than two years after the first certificate. BSI issued 11 certificates in 2025 and says the 2026 figure has more than tripled. Certified organisations span more than 16 countries and four regions: Japan, India, Australia, Thailand, Singapore and Vietnam in Asia Pacific; the UK, the Netherlands, France, Italy, Spain and Portugal in Europe; and Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Egypt in the Middle East and Africa. Sectors include financial services, technology and advisory firms; the release names no financial institution.
  • DIFC becomes the first financial centre certified to ISO/IEC 42001:2023 (DIFC press release, 2026-11-17): the Dubai International Financial Centre said that, following independent third-party certification, it had become the first financial centre in the world to be awarded ISO/IEC 42001:2023, across the AI User, AI Producer and AI Provider scopes. The release does not name the certification body.
  • A formal security analysis of the C2PA provenance standard (IACR ePrint 2026/804, received 2026-04-23, revised 2026-06-02): the paper “Verifying Provenance of Digital Media: Security Analysis of C2PA and its Implementation”, by researchers at the University of Maryland, Baltimore County (UMBC), Hacker Factor and the US National Security Agency (NSA), uses formal methods to examine C2PA specification version 2.2, validator implementations and the conformance programme version 0.1. The abstract states that conforming implementations fail to achieve both claimed and essential security goals; generators and validators fail to agree on the signature’s trusted timestamp; inadequate certificate revocation policies cause conforming validators to accept manifests they should not; conforming validators produce inconsistent results; the exclusion range enables undetectable alterations; and the conformance programme certifies products without technical review or defined requirements. The authors say some of their recommendations were adopted in the Pixel 10 Pro and C2PA version 2.3.
  • C2PA released Content Credentials 2.3 in February (C2PA news, 2026-02-09): the C2PA news page lists “The C2PA Launches Content Credentials 2.3” on 2026-02-09, describing it as the latest version of the open standard.
  • Draft second edition of ISO 30401 on knowledge management systems (DIN Media, 2026-06): DIN Media, the publishing arm of the German standards body, lists the draft international standard ISO/DIS 30401:2026-06 “Knowledge management systems — Requirements”, published June 2026, 27 pages.
